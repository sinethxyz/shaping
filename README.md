# shaping

### Tell intelligence what to become.

**A programmer works with instructions.**  
**A designer works with visual and interactive elements.**  
**A shaper works with intelligence itself.**

**SHAPING** is the creative practice of developing machine intelligence through intention, examples, experimentation, and refinement.

A person describes what they want a machine intelligence to become. They demonstrate, experiment, observe, test, and revise until its capabilities, behaviours, and boundaries better reflect that intention.

The person practising shaping is a **shaper**.

---

## A new medium for creation

| Medium | Human activity | What people create |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Language | Writing | Expressed ideas and knowledge |
| Computation | Programming | Executable software |
| Visual design | Designing | Visual and interactive experiences |
| 3D environments | Modelling | Digital objects and worlds |
| **Machine intelligence** | **Shaping** | **Intelligent capabilities and behaviours** |

This is a proposed conceptual framework, not a claim that the other practices disappear or that machine intelligence is a literal physical medium.

## From instructions to intentions

| Practice | Core question | Typical act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programming | How should this execute? | Construct instructions to produce behaviour |
| AI-assisted or “vibe” coding | What software should be built? | Describe desired software and iterate on an implementation |
| **Shaping** | **What should this intelligence become?** | Develop, observe, evaluate, and refine capabilities and boundaries |

These practices can overlap. Shaping is not defined by whether someone writes code.

## The shaping loop

**Intend → Demonstrate → Experiment → Observe → Evaluate → Refine → Preserve**

1. **Intend.** State the capability, behaviour, or outcome you seek.
2. **Demonstrate.** Provide examples, counterexamples, criteria, and boundaries.
3. **Experiment.** Make an intervention in the machine-intelligence system.
4. **Observe.** See what the system actually does, including unexpected behaviours.
5. **Evaluate.** Compare outcomes with the intention, including trade-offs and failures.
6. **Refine.** Change the system, its context, its examples, or its constraints.
7. **Preserve.** Keep the resulting configuration, evidence, and history as an evolving **Creation**.

Repeat as understanding improves. The loop is a starting model, not a mandatory interface.

## What is a Creation?

A **Creation** is a persistent, evolving work produced through shaping.

A Creation may contain selected models, prompts, adapters, learned weights, tools, routing rules, examples, evaluations, safety boundaries, configurations, and version history. It should be possible to inspect what changed and why.

A Creation is **not necessarily a newly trained foundation model**. The intelligence a person shapes may be realised through model adaptation, composition, retrieval, tooling, or other techniques. The implementation matters; the creative practice is not tied to one implementation.

## Shaping is not just prompting

One prompt can express an intention, but an isolated conversation is not necessarily a shaping practice. What distinguishes shaping is the sustained development of a capability or behaviour, with feedback and a work that can be revised, evaluated, and carried forward.

The expression **“works with intelligence itself”** names the creative object of the practice. It does not imply that machine systems are conscious or that every change modifies a model's parameters.

## Principles

- **Intention precedes implementation.** Start from what should become possible.
- **Examples are creative material.** Demonstrations and counterexamples help express intention.
- **Behaviour matters more than a successful run.** Observe failures, regressions, and edge cases.
- **Boundaries are part of the work.** What a system must not do is as important as what it can do.
- **Evidence over mythology.** Don't confuse plausible demonstrations with verified ability.
- **Iteration is the craft.** A shaper learns through experiments and refinement.
- **The work should endure.** Preserve configurations, findings, provenance, and versions.
- **Accessibility without pretending complexity has vanished.** People should be able to begin without specialist ML training while still seeing the relevant evidence and constraints.
