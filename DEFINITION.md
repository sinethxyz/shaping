# SHAPING — definition and scope

**Status:** Proposed definition, v0.1  
**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Initial proposer:** Sineth Madduma

## Definition

> **SHAPING is the creative practice of developing machine intelligence through intention, examples, experimentation, and refinement.**

A **shaper** is a person who intentionally develops the capabilities, behaviours, and boundaries of a machine-intelligence system by demonstrating desired outcomes, intervening in the system, observing the results, and iterating.

A **Creation** is the persistent, evolving artefact of that practice.

The central question is:

> **Is this becoming what I had in mind?**

This complements the engineering question:

> **Did this change work?**

A strong shaping process asks both.

## What qualifies?

A project can be understood as shaping when it has most of these characteristics:

1. A stated intention for a machine-intelligence capability or behaviour.
2. Examples, preferences, criteria, or boundaries that make the intention operational.
3. An intervention that may change observed behaviour.
4. Observation and feedback over successive attempts.
5. Evaluation of what improved and what worsened.
6. A result that can be preserved, revised, or transferred beyond a single exchange.

The practice can be highly technical or accessible through tools. It can be individual or collaborative.

## Mechanisms, not identities

Shaping may use prompting, retrieval, instruction design, datasets, fine-tuning, distillation, reinforcement learning, evaluation, agent architectures, composition, model selection, and tool integration. These are possible **means** of shaping, not interchangeable operations and not all necessarily available in any given product.

Calling an activity shaping does not prove that underlying model weights changed. A useful Creation could be a composed or configured system rather than a new model.

## Boundaries

- **Not a synonym for prompt engineering.** Prompting can be part of shaping, but shaping implies a continuing process of developing a capability or behaviour.
- **Not a synonym for machine learning.** Some shaping may involve learning or training; other shaping may alter orchestration, context, constraints, or tools.
- **Not merely software generation.** A shaper's direct concern is how an intelligent system behaves and what it can do, even where software is part of the result.
- **Not a claim of consciousness.** “Intelligence” here refers pragmatically to machine capabilities and behaviours.
- **Not a claim of novelty for existing techniques.** The proposal is a framing of the human practice. Related terminology and technical work predate it.
- **Not a substitute for verification.** Intended behaviour must not be confused with demonstrated behaviour.

## Working distinction

A programmer asks: **How should the machine execute this?**

A designer asks: **How should this experience look and behave?**

A shaper asks: **What capability or behaviour should this intelligence develop, and how can I know it has?**

People can be all three, and the practices overlap.

## Open questions

This document is a proposal meant to improve with use. Areas for research include:

- What is the minimum durable artefact of shaping?
- How should a Creation represent provenance and evidence?
- How can evaluation remain understandable to non-specialists?
- When does a configured system count as distinct from its source model?
- How should shared Creations record consent, rights, data provenance, and attribution?
- How should the activity evolve when capabilities or safety constraints change?

**The ambition is to make developing machine intelligence a human creative activity without hiding the responsibility that comes with it.**
