# Shaping glossary

**Status: proposed terminology, not an industry standard.**

### SHAPING

The creative practice of developing machine intelligence through intention, examples, experimentation, and refinement.

### Shaper

A person engaged in shaping: someone who deliberately develops and revises machine-intelligence capabilities, behaviours, and boundaries.

### Creation

A persistent, evolving work produced through shaping. It contains enough information to represent, revisit, test, and further develop an intended intelligent capability or behaviour.

### Intention

The desired capability, behaviour, or outcome a shaper wants to develop, including constraints and trade-offs.

### Demonstration

An example or counterexample used to communicate intention. Demonstrations can be provided in natural language, media, structured data, interactive tasks, or other forms.

### Intervention

A deliberate change that may affect the behaviour of a machine-intelligence system: for example a configuration, tool, retrieval source, model, training process, or orchestration change.

### Evaluation

A structured attempt to determine whether a Creation behaves as intended, and where it fails or regresses. Evaluation can combine quantitative tests, qualitative review, and human judgment.

### Boundary

A restriction, failure condition, safety requirement, or other constraint that helps define acceptable behaviour.

### Provenance

The record of where a Creation's constituent parts came from, what was changed, and on what basis claims about its behaviour are made.

### Version

A preserved state of a Creation, ideally accompanied by its changes and evidence.

## Terminology principle

The vocabulary should help people discuss and practise shaping. It should not become a barrier to participation. Existing technical terms retain their established meanings; these definitions do not purport to rename or replace them.
